FRC Net Mineral Acres By State

85,583 of our 104,000 net mineral acres sit in Oklahoma — roughly 82% of the position, with the balance spread across Texas, Arkansas, Colorado, New Mexico and other states.

Buying minerals

From 1928 through 1932 Farmers exchanged 1 share of stock for 30 acres of fee minerals, acquiring 43,000 net mineral acres. These are our “Original” acres.

Farmers started buying minerals in 1983. Since then we have purchased over 63,000 net mineral acres in 102 transactions, for $5.3 million dollars. These are our “Purchased” acres.

Our average cost per acre for all purchases is $78.

Our two most successful purchases

Petrocorp, 1995. We paid $2,150,000 for 32,816 net acres and have made over $92,000,000 in oil and gas revenue.

Oryx, 1993. We paid $350,000 for 9,832 net acres and have made over $34,000,000 in oil and gas revenue.

Revenue from purchased minerals

69% — $78.7 million dollars — of our oil and gas revenue for the last 10 years has come from our purchased minerals.

70% — $7.3 million dollars — of our lease bonus income for the last 10 years has come from our purchased minerals.

Cooperative background

Farmers was organized in 1929. The idea behind the company was that a farmer would exchange 30 acres of fee minerals for one share of stock. Originally 1,600+ people exchanged approximately 43,000 net mineral acres for stock in the company. As a cooperative that meant that no matter where a well was drilled, all shareholders would benefit.

Our goal

Our goal has always been to pay the largest dividend possible while maintaining sufficient cash for the long term growth of the company by acquiring minerals and participating in wells. We have no debt and have self funded all of our acquisitions. Having this strong cash position has allowed us to consistently establish a dividend per share that we could maintain through periods of low oil and gas prices.

Stock splits

In 2007 the last share of stock sold was sold for over $52,000. The Board recognized that this price made the shares virtually unsellable, especially for people having to sell their stock because of estate issues. In 2008 the Board recommended, and the shareholders approved, an 8 to 1 stock split. In 2017, again due to the high price of our stock, the Board recommended, and the shareholders approved, a 10 to 1 stock split.
